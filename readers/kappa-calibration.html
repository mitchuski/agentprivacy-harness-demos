κ calibration: what a CPU-second is worth

A compute-priced claim needs a unit. The lane counted instructions with callgrind on the real charged models at a fixed 20,000-conflict budget (output identical to native), then timed the same replays idle, under a busy-loop load, saturated (the charged condition) and at four threads. The rate, and so the price κ, is a function of load: about 2^32 idle against about 2^30.0 to 2^30.6 saturated. Every number here is recomputed in your browser from 171 minted rows; status kappa-compatible (unverified).

What the lane did with it

The original calibration (one call, loaded) gave 2^30.03 instructions per CPU-second; with 2224 ops per unit and an ops-per-instruction factor the lane priced κ = 2^24 per CPU-second, then 2^23 (crediting a rival's re-justification), then per phase 2^22.5 to 2^22.8 from the saturated maxima above. The per-phase version was measured and not shipped: the κ lever was exhausted when the coordinator changed plan to the blind route search. The point of the table is the spread: idle versus saturated is a factor of about 4, which is 2 bits of claim, and a judge that cannot see the load cannot tell which one you charged. Minted beside the ledger it was charged against, the calibration becomes a fact a reviewer can re-derive, not a sentence in a proof.
